Nine Suggestions on How to Get the Most Out of This Book
by Dale Carnegie

1. Develop a Deep, Driving Desire to Learn
If you wish to get the most out of this book, there is one indispensable requirement, one essential infinitely more important than any rule or technique: a deep, driving desire to learn, a vigorous determination to increase your ability to deal with people.  

How to develop this urge: Constantly remind yourself how important these principles are to you. Picture to yourself how their mastery will aid you in leading a richer, fuller, happier, and more fulfilling life.

Mantra to repeat:

"My popularity, my happiness and sense of worth depend to no small extent upon my skill in dealing with people."

2. Read Each Chapter Twice
Read each chapter rapidly at first to get a bird's-eye view of it. Unless you are reading merely for entertainment, do not rush to the next chapter immediately. Go back and reread each chapter thoroughly. In the long run, this will save time and yield actual results.

3. Pause and Reflect Frequently
Stop frequently in your reading to think over what you are reading. Ask yourself just how and when you can apply each suggestion.

4. Read Actively with a Marker
Read with a crayon, pencil, pen, magic marker, or highlighter in your hand.

Draw a line beside any suggestion you feel you can use.

For a four-star suggestion, underscore every sentence, highlight it, or mark it with "****".

Marking and underscoring a book makes it more interesting and far easier to review rapidly later.

5. Review the Book Monthly
The rapidity with which we forget is astonishing. Skimming through this book once will not suffice.

Monthly Review: Spend a few hours reviewing the book every month.

Keep It Visible: Keep it on your desk in front of you every day and glance through it often.

Form Habits: Constant and vigorous review and application are the only ways to make these principles habitual.

6. Apply the Principles Daily
Bernard Shaw once remarked: "If you teach a man anything, he will never learn." Shaw was right. Learning is an active process—we learn by doing.

Forming New Habits: Remember that you are attempting a new way of life, which requires time, persistence, and daily application.

Whenever you face a specific problem—like handling a child, winning a spouse to your way of thinking, or satisfying an irritated customer—avoid the natural, impulsive reaction. Instead, turn to these pages, review the underscored paragraphs, and apply the new methods.

7. Make Learning a Game
Offer your spouse, your child, or a business associate a dime or a dollar every time they catch you violating a certain principle. Turn mastering these rules into a lively, interactive game.

8. Conduct Weekly Self-Examinations
A prominent Wall Street banker built a highly efficient self-improvement system by dedicating Saturday evenings to review:

Isolate Yourself: Set aside quiet time to open your appointment book and reflect on the week's interviews, discussions, and meetings.

Ask Three Questions:

"What mistakes did I make that time?"

"What did I do that was right—and in what way could I have improved my performance?"

"What lessons can I learn from that experience?"

Using a weekly check-up system like this will make your educational process intriguing and dramatically expand your ability to deal with people.

9. Keep a Record of Your Triumphs
Use the blank pages at the end of this book to record your successes in applying these principles. Be specific—include names, dates, and results. Keeping a log will inspire greater efforts and serve as a fascinating memory to stumble upon years down the road.
